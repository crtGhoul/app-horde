0 · what is sideloading


☠ READ THIS FIRST

This site is an index, not a host. {{GUIDE_DISCLAIMER_SRC}} — I don't make, modify, or scan those files, and I can't vouch for any of them.


Do your own research before installing anything: confirm the bundle ID matches the real app ·
check which source it came from and how fresh the build is · google the tweak name for scam reports ·
never sideload banking, payment, or 2FA apps.


Not affiliated with Apple, the repos, or any app developer. Tweaked apps can be revoked, get accounts
banned, or be outright malware. Everything here is use-at-your-own-risk.



  
Normally your iPhone only installs apps from Apple's App Store. Sideloading means
  installing app files (.ipa files) from outside the App Store. The apps in this horde are
  tweaked builds — the original apps with mods like unlocked features or removed ads,
  uploaded by the repos.

  
You need an installer app on your iPhone to sign and install them. Three ways to do it below —
  pick the one that fits you.

5 · survival rules



  
① Use a spare Apple ID for signing — never your main one.

  
② Free Apple IDs expire every 7 days. Re-open AltStore/SideStore on Wi-Fi to refresh before apps die.

  
③ Free/shared certificates get revoked. If an app suddenly won't open, the cert died — re-sign it.

  
④ Tweaked ≠ official. Star ratings here verify the original app, not the modified build. Mods can get accounts banned.

  
⑤ Never sideload banking, payment, or 2FA apps from anywhere but the App Store.

  
⑥ If an app asks for logins it shouldn't need, delete it. When in doubt, check the uploader and the FRESH badge.





6 · when things break


// the errors every beginner hits, and the fix for each



  
✗ "Unable to Verify App"Your certificate was revoked or expired. Re-sign the app: in Scarlet re-import your certificate and reinstall; in AltStore/SideStore open the app and refresh. Free/shared certificates die often — this is normal.

  
✗ App installs but crashes instantlyThree usual causes: dead certificate (fix above), the build needs a newer iOS than you have (check REQUIRES iOS in the app's intel), or the tweak itself is broken. Try an older build from the version picker in the app's intel.

  
✗ AltStore says "Could not find AltServer"Your iPhone and computer must be on the same Wi-Fi, AltServer must be running, and iTunes (from Apple, not the Microsoft Store) must see your phone. Plug in with a cable once to re-pair.

  
✗ AltServer doesn't see my iPhoneUnlock the phone, tap Trust on both ends, and make sure Wi-Fi sync is enabled in iTunes/Finder. Restart AltServer if it still hides.

  
✗ "Developer Mode Required" (iOS 16+)Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Developer Mode, turn it on, and restart when asked. Then trust the profile again.

  
✗ Scarlet / ESign itself won't openIts own certificate got revoked by Apple. Wait for the service to issue a new one, or switch to AltStore/SideStore with your own Apple ID.

  
✗ "App integrity could not be verified"Same as a revoked certificate — re-sign and reinstall the app.

  
✗ TrollStore won't install an appCheck your iOS version: TrollStore only works on 14.0–17.0, never 17.0.1+. Also use .tipa builds (marked TIPA in the version picker) for TrollStore-only apps.

  
✗ Download link is deadTry the other source's button in the app's intel, or pick an older build from the version picker. Repo CDNs move files around.

  
✗ "You hit the 3-app limit"Free Apple IDs can only keep 3 sideloaded apps active. Delete one in AltStore/SideStore to make room, or look into LiveContainer to bundle more.





7 · verify before you install


// every app's intel panel runs these checks for you — here's what they mean, and what to check yourself



  
① Open the app's intel and read the VERIFICATION row. It automatically checks three things: whether the bundle ID matches a real App Store listing, how many independent repos carry the app, and whether the repo's build version lines up with the App Store's current release.

  
② Bundle ID must match. A ✓ means the ID belongs to the real app. ✗ means no App Store record exists — treat it with extra suspicion or skip it.

  
③ More repos beats one. An app listed by several independent repos is a weak positive signal. A lone upload to a single repo deserves a harder look.

  
④ The version must make sense. If a repo build claims a newer version than the App Store currently has, walk away — that's a classic fake. Older builds are usually just outdated.

  
⑤ Check the tweak name. The TWEAKS row says what's modified. Google "tweak name malware" and "tweak name scam" before installing anything that handles logins or money.

  
⑥ After downloading, check the file. A real .ipa is tens to hundreds of MB — a 2 MB "Spotify" is not Spotify. When your installer asks you to trust a certificate, make sure you recognize whose it is.

  
⑦ Nothing here proves safety. These checks catch fakes and sloppiness, not a well-made trojan. If an app asks for permissions or logins it shouldn't need, delete it.
